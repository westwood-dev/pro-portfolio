## A browser-native 3D gallery, built when the physical one was cancelled

When lockdown ended my foundation course early, the final show — the thing a year of work builds towards — disappeared with it. So I built a replacement: a fully 3D gallery you walk through in the browser, holding the work of 50 students and tutors. No app, no download, just a link. It opened with a private view over Zoom and had over 300 visitors in its first month.

![Gallery interior](/images/unlocked-gallery/GallerySS1-min.webp)

The space is modelled on the Boros Bunker in Berlin, which our class had visited weeks before everything shut. It holds a real range of work — video, photography, 3D scans — hung across the rooms as you move through them.

![Gallery interior](/images/unlocked-gallery/GallerySS2-min.webp)

### How it's built

The space was designed in Blender and brought into the browser with Babylon.js. The real constraint was reach: it had to run on whatever laptop a visitor happened to own, not a gaming rig. Every asset was converted to the `dxt.ktx` compressed texture format to cut GPU memory right down, so the whole gallery stays smooth on low-powered hardware.

![Gallery view](/images/unlocked-gallery/GallerySS3-min.webp)

Visitors could also buy high-quality copies of collections — every penny raised went to a range of Black Lives Matter charities.
