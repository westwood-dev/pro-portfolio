## Art installation exploring the concept of existence in the digital realm

*CCI Diploma Degree Show, South London - 07/2023*

Everyone assumes their data is personal, that somewhere, a system knows them. This installation breaks that assumption quietly: it doesn't recognise you, it just assigns you an identity and remembers which one it gave you.

#### How it works

A webcam scans your face. Facial recognition doesn't identify who you are, it only checks whether it's seen you before. First visit, it generates a full synthetic identity: a StyleGAN2 face, a custom-GAN fingerprint, a Faker-built profile of personal data. It prints the lot as a receipt, styled like real surveillance output. Come back, and you get the exact same “you", proof the system is tracking, even though nothing about it is real.

#### Why fake data

Real user data was never an option, practically or ethically, and using it would have missed the point. The piece isn't about your data, it's about a system's confidence that it has it. Fabricated data that looks convincingly real does that job better than genuine data would.

#### Build

- MediaPipe + OpenCV for face detection and re-identification - fast enough to feel instant, which matters for the moment of recognition to land
- StyleGAN2 for faces, a custom-built GAN for fingerprints - different generators for different levels of biometric plausibility
- Faker (Python) for the surrounding personal data
- iPad for the interaction, thermal printer for the receipt, 24" monitor for ambient output - a deliberately unglamorous, official-looking rig, so the receipt reads as bureaucratic fact rather than art

#### What happened

Visitors who returned got visibly shocked seeing the same fake data printed back at them, the illusion of being "known" worked. But the response that mattered more: people started comparing receipts with friends, laughing at the invented details. The piece triggered curiosity and comparison, not the discomfort of being surveilled. In hindsight, that's a finding about the piece, not a failure of it. People don't fear data collection until the system appears to judge them with it, not just hold it.

#### What I'd change

Make the system assumptive, not just retentive. Right now it proves it remembers you, the real threat of mass surveillance is systems that infer and judge: risk scores, character assumptions, decisions made about you from data you never gave consent to shape. That's the version of this piece that would land as fear rather than novelty.
